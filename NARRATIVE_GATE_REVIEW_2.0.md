# Narrative gate review — Revision 2.0

The level redesign was not approved until the story passed this gate.

## Character agency

**Pip:** Makes the final moral choice and becomes a different kind of Keeper. Pass.

**Sable:** Has a coherent constructive objective—ending centralized authority—and her criticism permanently changes the settlement. Pass.

**Rowan:** His concealment causes the present crisis; he confesses and loses authority. Pass.

**Juniper:** Is revealed as the child Tovin saved, defines the story's ethic of care, and entrusts Pip with the Act III path. Pass.

**Lumi:** Aids Sable, confesses, and later uses Sable's teaching to defeat Sable's decoys. Pass.

**Tovin:** His action—giving away the mark—is the missing causal fact, not a passive tragedy. Pass.

## Plot causality

- Sable steals Promises in the order of Tovin's last route. Pass.
- Every missing Promise creates the next playable conflict. Pass.
- Major reveals are foreshadowed by the transferred mark, Rowan's language, Juniper's satchel, and Tovin's repeated verb. Pass.
- The midpoint changes the player's understanding of both antagonist and institution. Pass.
- The climax is resolved by the same priority reasoning the player taught. Pass.

## Thematic integrity

The story does not argue that rules are inherently good or inherently oppressive. It distinguishes a revisable public promise from unquestioned authority. Sable's valid criticism survives the defeat of her method. The original Keeper monopoly is not restored. Pass.

## Emotional escalation

The progression moves from wonder and immediate peril, to mystery, to betrayal, to a personal moral test, to a community-scale choice. Failure consequences are visually and narratively distinct. Pass.

## Final approval

The story is approved as the foundation for the twelve-level chapter. The levels were redesigned only after this review passed.
